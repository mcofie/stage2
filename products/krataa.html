01 / The Problem

Business runs on email, but owners aren't at a desk

Orders, invoices, bookings and bank documents arrive by email. For a shop owner in Accra, they arrive while they're at the market, on the road, or out of data. By the time the email is read, the customer has often asked someone else.

Most small businesses also write from a personal address like kofiscakes1987@gmail.com. Suppliers, banks and big companies take an address with the business name more seriously, but getting one usually means a bank card and technical setup.

Missed while offline

Important emails land while the owner has no data, and nothing tells them until they next open their inbox.

Looking small

A Gmail address makes an established business look like a side project to banks, suppliers and tenders.

Card-only, technical setup

Domains and business email are usually paid by card and configured by hand: two barriers for many small businesses.

One inbox, many hands

Orders come from the website, Instagram and marketplaces, and more than one person needs to answer them.

02 / The Solution

Your own address, and an SMS when it matters

Krataa means "letter" in Twi. As the site puts it, a letter with your name on it gets opened, read and answered. Krataa sets up a .com that fits the business name, takes payment by mobile money, and sends one SMS for every email from an important sender, with a link to read and reply.

SMS works on any phone and any network, with no internet, so the alert gets through even when data doesn't. Owners who aren't ready for their own domain can start free, keep their Gmail, and still get alerts.
