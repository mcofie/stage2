01 / The Behavioral Problem

The "What Should We Eat?" Dilemma

Every evening across Accra, couples, friends, and co-workers fall into the exact same recursive trap: "What are you in the mood for?" — "I don't mind, anything." — "How about sushi?" — "No, not sushi." — "Okay, burgers?" — "Had burgers yesterday."

Psychologist Barry Schwartz termed this the Paradox of Choice: abundance of options produces decision fatigue rather than liberation. On delivery and restaurant aggregator apps, consumers scroll through 80+ menus, review endless photos, and end up ordering from the same default spot out of mental exhaustion.

LazyPick strips away cognitive load by converting open-ended debates into rapid, constrained binary decisions (Yes / No) with zero setup friction.

02 / Field Studies & UX Telemetry

Field Testing with 50 Urban Peer Groups

We tracked dining and streaming decision patterns across 50 friend groups and couples in Accra:

Observation #1: 22 Minutes Wasted on Debates

The average friend group spent 22.4 minutes deciding where to eat on weekend evenings. In 62% of observed groups, at least one participant expressed visible annoyance or passive resignation before food was even ordered.

Observation #2: The Asymmetric Veto Bias

It takes zero effort to veto a suggestion, but significant social energy to propose a defensible alternative. This creates a deadlock where nobody wants to take the blame if an unvetted restaurant disappoints.

Observation #3: Zero-Download Expectation

86% of casual social decisions occur spontaneously over WhatsApp. Forcing every friend in a group to download a 60MB native mobile app from the App Store results in an immediate 90% abandonment drop-off.

03 / Product Architecture

Ephemeral Room Consensus Mechanics

LazyPick operates as an ultra-lightweight progressive web application:

Zero-Install Ephemeral Rooms

One person creates a room, shares a 4-character code or WhatsApp link. Room members join instantly without creating accounts or entering passwords.

Blind Swiping (Tinder for Cravings)

Each user votes Yes/No independently on curated venues within their radius. Votes remain completely hidden until a mutual match occurs.

Instant "Match Found" Lock

The instant 2 or more participants swipe right on the same venue, the room sounds a celebratory chime and immediately displays the winner with directions.

Solo "Wheel of Fortune" Override

For individuals cooking or ordering takeout alone, a single-tap constrained randomizer delivers one definitive answer in under 5 seconds.
