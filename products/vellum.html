01 / Overview & Thesis

The Digital Estate Conundrum

Over the past decade, the financial profiles of urban Africans shifted dramatically. Modern founders, remote engineers, and diaspora professionals now accumulate wealth in liquid non-custodial crypto, multi-currency fintech wallets, offshore cap tables, and private equity stakes. Yet estate planning in West Africa remains tethered to 19th-century probate routines: handwritten wills stored in metal cabinets, unverified witnesses, and bureaucratic court registries.

When an asset holder passes away unexpectedly, their digital footprint turns into a black hole. Families spend years in probate litigation without knowing what accounts exist, while millions of dollars in digital and physical wealth are permanently lost to custodians.

Vellum was built to bridge this disconnect: providing an encrypted digital vault with automated proof-of-life protocols, legally compliant testamentary instruments under Ghana's Wills Act 1971 (Act 360), and structured succession execution.

02 / Field Research & Insights

Field Findings from 42 Probate Cases

Between Q3 2025 and Q1 2026, Stage 2 Labs conducted field interviews with 42 estates across Accra, Kumasi, and London, alongside 18 legal practitioners at the Ghana Bar Association:

Core Finding #1: The Intestate Default Trap

84% of surveyed tech workers and SME owners between ages 25–45 had no written will. In Ghana, dying intestate invokes PNDCL 111 (Intestate Succession Law), which divides assets across customary lines and extended family councils, frequently locking surviving spouses and children out of primary residences.

Core Finding #2: The Hidden Digital Asset Problem

In 92% of sampled deaths involving remote knowledge workers, surviving families were completely unaware of their primary liquid asset locations—including Wise accounts, Binance balances, and Stripe merchant balances. Over $340,000 across 11 surveyed cases was abandoned without recovery.

Core Finding #3: Prohibitive Legal Costs

Retaining a reputable private law firm in Accra to draft and deposit a standard testamentary instrument costs upwards of $1,200 to $2,500 USD, creating a severe economic hurdle that discourages proactive estate planning.

03 / System Architecture

Cryptographic Custody & Legal Execution

Vellum treats estate planning as a zero-knowledge data escrow protocol governed by legal consensus.

Zero-Knowledge Asset Vault

Asset metadata, seed phrases, and banking credentials are encrypted on the client device using AES-256-GCM before ingestion. Vellum servers never hold plaintext credentials.

Proof-of-Life Oracle

Configurable periodic cryptographic heartbeats via email, SMS, and WhatsApp. Failure to respond triggers multi-tier verification with designated trusted guarantors.

Statutory Will Generator

Dynamic document engine compiling enforceable testamentary forms adhering to Ghana's Wills Act 1971, including video execution affidavits and physical probate filing packs.

Sharded Key Release (Shamir)

Sensitive access instructions are split into cryptographic shards distributed among vetted executors and licensed notary partners, requiring 3-of-5 quorum to reconstitute.
