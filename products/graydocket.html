01 / The Problem

Registering a business shouldn't mean a week of office visits

For a first-time founder in Ghana, formalising a business means a chain of steps across institutions: checking that a name is available at the Office of the Registrar of Companies (ORC), preparing and filing the registration, getting a TIN, and then remembering recurring obligations like annual returns and name renewals.

Each step is easy to get wrong when you are doing it for the first time, and mistakes are costly: a rejected name sends you back to the start, and a missed deadline quietly puts the business out of good standing.

Name rejections

Exact matches and look-alike names in the ORC register get applications rejected after you have already filed.

Forms and queues

Printed forms, in-person visits and unclear status updates make a simple registration feel opaque.

Compliance after day one

Annual returns, renewals and tax registrations recur every year, and it is easy to lose track while running the business.

Scattered documents

Certificates, constitutions and TIN certificates end up spread across email, paper folders and phones.

02 / The Solution

Registration and compliance as one service

GrayDocket turns registration into a 15-minute questionnaire. The team checks the details, files with the ORC, and posts every update to a dashboard. Once the certificate is issued, the same account keeps tracking what is due next.

It supports sole proprietorships, companies limited by shares, and companies limited by guarantee (non-profits). Existing businesses can be added too, so their filings are tracked from then on.
